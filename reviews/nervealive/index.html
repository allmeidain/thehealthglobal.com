What Is NerveAlive And How Does It Work?

The burning in your feet isn't damage happening. It's a cleanup crew that forgot to go home.

Here's the part almost nobody explains. Your nerves have their own maintenance team, a set of cells called microglia. Their whole job is night work: patch the small tears, clear out what's broken, leave before morning. In a healthy body you never feel them working at all.

Now picture that same crew after years of stress, poor sleep, and thin nutrition. Nobody calls them off. So they keep working a road that's already fine — tearing up good pavement, laying cones on a street with no potholes left in it. The road is permanently under construction, and you feel every bit of it. Pins and needles at 2 a.m. Toes that go numb in a chair. That electric zap up the calf that arrives with no warning at all.

Call it the repair crew that won't clock out. It reframes the whole problem, because a crew working overtime isn't damage — it's a signal nobody turned off. And the research this formula draws from, out of groups at Indiana University, the University of Michigan, and the Turku Centre for Biotechnology, points at exactly that overactivity as the thing sitting under the pain.

NerveAlive is a daily capsule aimed at the crew, not at the noise it makes. Two things happen at once. It closes the nutritional gaps that leave nerve tissue running short in the first place, and it supplies therapeutic nutrients selected to quiet that overactive microglial signalling and support normal central nerve function.

That's a different target than a numbing agent. Numbing turns down your ability to feel the construction. This is about getting the crew to pack up.

Read it as a set and the design is obvious. Nothing here is trying to mask a signal. Every group is aimed at the crew that keeps generating it.
